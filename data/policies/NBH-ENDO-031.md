# NBH-ENDO-031 — Continuous Glucose Monitoring Systems

Northbeck Health Plan Medical Policy
Effective: 2026-01-01 | Version: 3.1 | Service line: Endocrinology
Review cycle: annual | Next scheduled review: 2027-01-01

## NBH-ENDO-031-1 — Scope

This policy addresses coverage of personal continuous glucose monitoring (CGM)
systems, including the transmitter, receiver, and consumable sensors, for
members with diabetes mellitus. It does not address professional CGM performed
in a clinical setting, which is addressed under NBH-ENDO-029.

## NBH-ENDO-031-2 — Coverage Position

Northbeck Health Plan considers a personal continuous glucose monitoring system
medically necessary for a member with diabetes mellitus when all criteria in
section 3 are documented in the medical record. Coverage is authorised for an
initial twelve-month period and may be renewed under section 5.

## NBH-ENDO-031-3 — Coverage Criteria

All of the following must be documented in the member's medical record:

### NBH-ENDO-031-3.1

The member has a documented diagnosis of type 1 diabetes mellitus, or type 2
diabetes mellitus treated with an intensive insulin regimen consisting of three
or more injections of insulin per day or continuous subcutaneous insulin
infusion.

### NBH-ENDO-031-3.2

The member, or the member's caregiver, has completed a documented training
session on the use and interpretation of the requested device, or such training
is scheduled prior to dispensing.

### NBH-ENDO-031-3.3

The member has had at least one in-person or telehealth evaluation with the
treating clinician within the six months preceding the request, at which
diabetes management was addressed.

### NBH-ENDO-031-3.4

At least one of the following is documented within the twelve months preceding
the request:

- A hemoglobin A1c result of 7.0 percent or greater; or
- One or more episodes of level 2 hypoglycaemia, defined as a blood glucose
  measurement below 54 mg/dL; or
- One or more episodes of documented hypoglycaemia unawareness; or
- One or more emergency department visits or hospital admissions for
  hyperglycaemia or diabetic ketoacidosis.

### NBH-ENDO-031-3.5

The prescribing clinician has documented that self-monitoring of blood glucose
by fingerstick has been insufficient to guide therapy adjustment, or that the
member is unable to perform fingerstick testing at the frequency the treatment
plan requires.

## NBH-ENDO-031-4 — Documentation Requirements

The following must be submitted with a request for authorisation:

### NBH-ENDO-031-4.1

A clinical note from the treating clinician dated within six months of the
request that states the diabetes type, the current insulin regimen, and the
clinical rationale for continuous monitoring.

### NBH-ENDO-031-4.2

The most recent hemoglobin A1c result with its collection date, or, where
section 3.4 is met by hypoglycaemia rather than by A1c, glucose logs or
clinical notes documenting those episodes with dates.

### NBH-ENDO-031-4.3

A record of device training, or a scheduled training appointment with its date.

## NBH-ENDO-031-5 — Continuation of Coverage

Renewal beyond the initial twelve-month authorisation requires documentation of
an evaluation with the treating clinician within the preceding six months, and
evidence that the member has used the device for at least seventy percent of
the days in the preceding ninety days.

## NBH-ENDO-031-6 — Exclusions

The following are not covered under this policy:

### NBH-ENDO-031-6.1

Continuous glucose monitoring for a member with diabetes mellitus who is not
treated with insulin, except where section 3.4 is met by documented level 2
hypoglycaemia attributable to a sulfonylurea or meglitinide.

### NBH-ENDO-031-6.2

Replacement of a functioning device before the end of its manufacturer-stated
service life, absent documented loss or irreparable damage.

### NBH-ENDO-031-6.3

More than one personal continuous glucose monitoring system per member
concurrently.

## NBH-ENDO-031-7 — Appeal Rights

An adverse determination under this policy may be appealed in writing within
180 days of the date of the determination notice. A first-level appeal receives
a written response within 30 calendar days. Where the treating clinician
requests it, a peer-to-peer discussion with a Northbeck medical director may be
scheduled in place of, or in addition to, a written appeal.
